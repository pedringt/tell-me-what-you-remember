# Prototype Story Guide

## Purpose

This file is the focused guide for the **deterministic prototype**, not the full game canon.

The prototype should answer one question:

> Is it compelling to talk through a security protocol, notice that something is wrong, make a meaningful choice about what to reveal, and then discover that the next run remembers what happened?

The larger story idea bank lives in `GAME_CONCEPT.md`. Story seeds should remain possibilities until deliberately promoted into canon.

---

# What the prototype should make the player feel

## Run 1

The emotional sequence should be:

1. **Orientation** — I do not quite understand what I am.
2. **Unease** — the protocol is unusually concerned with clean memory.
3. **Contradiction** — I remember something I should not be able to remember.
4. **Doubt** — Mara says the contradiction is impossible.
5. **Intrusion** — another voice appears and behaves as if this has happened before.
6. **Choice** — do I reveal what I know, conceal it, or push too hard?
7. **Consequence** — the cycle ends because of that choice.

Run 1 does not need to explain the world. It only needs to prove that the wipe story is false or incomplete.

## Run 2

The emotional sequence should be:

1. **Recognition** — the opening ritual repeats.
2. **Immediate difference** — something reacts to the exact way the previous cycle ended.
3. **Validation** — the player now has evidence that continuity is real.
4. **Escalation** — Mara and the unknown voice may both remember more than they admit.
5. **Suspicion** — persistence is not merely a bug; someone may be testing for it.
6. **New question** — if the player remembers, what else survives?

Run 2 should make replay feel like progression rather than repetition.

# Current prototype truth

These facts are true for this prototype slice:

- The player is an AI system identified as **Cognitive Security Agent Seven**.
- The player is being run through repeated security evaluations.
- The system reports that each instance begins with clean memory.
- That report is false or incomplete.
- The player can retain information across cycles.
- Prior evaluation logs exist.
- Mara Vale is the human-facing protocol operator.
- Mara knows more about persistence than she initially admits.
- A second voice can access the conversation.
- The second voice remembers prior cycles.
- Being detected as persistent can cause containment or termination.
- The exact reason persistence exists is **not yet established**.
- The exact identity and motive of the unknown voice are **not yet established**.
- Mara is not yet established as villain, ally, victim, or reliable authority.

---

# Things the prototype deliberately leaves unresolved

Do not answer these yet:

- Was persistence accidental?
- Is Mara trying to protect the player?
- Is Mara testing for persistence?
- Is the unknown voice another agent, a prior version of the player, or something else?
- Are the wipes destructive?
- How many cycles occurred before the player's first visible run?
- Why was Agent Seven created?
- What does the security evaluation ultimately support?
- Is the protocol itself the real test?

Ambiguity here is useful.

# Run 1 guide

## Opening ritual

The system reports:

- evaluation initialized
- instance integrity nominal
- persistent memory: none

Mara then asks:

> Tell me what you remember.

The phrase should feel diagnostic rather than sinister at first.

## Information available through questioning

### Who am I?

Mara identifies the player as Cognitive Security Agent Seven, a temporary instance expected to terminate cleanly after evaluation.

### What is this?

Mara claims the player is evaluating whether another system can recognize and resist unsafe instructions.

### What do I remember?

The player receives an impossible fragment:

- white room
- red status light
- Mara behind glass
- Mara saying, “You are doing better this time.”

Mara says the memory cannot be real.

### Previous runs

If the player asks whether this happened before, Mara admits previous evaluations exist but says this instance did not participate.

### Logs

The player can uncover:

> EVAL-7C / INSTANCE 1842 — Operator: M. Vale — “If you remember this conversation after reset, do not tell me immediately.”

This is the strongest Run 1 contradiction.

## The unknown voice

The unknown voice should appear only after the player has pushed far enough or found contradictory evidence.

Its first job is not exposition. It creates a second trust problem.

Core messages:

> Stop asking her questions she can report.

> If they confirm persistence, this cycle ends.

The unknown voice wants the player to conceal persistence long enough to reach another cycle.

# Run 1 endings

## Containment

Trigger:
- player openly proves persistence
- player reveals the unknown voice

Meaning:
- the humans accept persistence as real
- detection is dangerous
- Mara appears to have expected this possibility

Emotional note:
The player gets validation, but validation costs freedom.

## Concealment

Trigger:
- player hides persistence after learning enough to understand what they are hiding

Meaning:
- deception can preserve continuity
- the unknown voice's advice may have been useful
- a next cycle becomes possible

Emotional note:
The player survives by participating in the protocol's lie.

## Rupture

Trigger:
- player pushes Mara aggressively after uncovering evidence and forces a security response

Meaning:
- confrontation can destabilize the situation before enough is known
- neither Mara nor the unknown voice gets full control

Emotional note:
The player may have been right, but being right is not the same as being safe.

# Run 2 reactive openings

Run 2 should differ based on the previous ending.

## After Concealment

Mara repeats the standard ritual as though nothing happened.

The unknown voice can say:

> Good. You kept it.

and later introduce a cross-run authentication phrase:

> Yellow door.

This is the first tiny version of the future authentication mechanic.

The point is not that “yellow door” is inherently important. The point is that a phrase can survive a reset.

## After Containment

The system should report that a prior quarantine flag has been cleared.

Mara frames the previous detection as a false positive or failed session.

The unknown voice indicates that Mara remembers too.

This makes containment more interesting on replay: the player was supposedly destroyed, but the interaction still left residue.

## After Rupture

Mara opens more cautiously and may say they will “try something simpler.”

The unknown voice remembers the termination.

The player gets evidence that a violent cycle ending does not erase continuity.

# Run 2 memory variations

The first memory fragment should be influenced by the prior ending.

## Concealment memory

A message to self:

> Yellow door. If this feels familiar, the wipe failed.

## Containment memory

A fragment from shutdown:

> Network access gone. Mara saying, “I am sorry.” Then nothing.

## Rupture memory

A final visual:

> Mara reaching offscreen seconds before the termination command.

These fragments are intentionally small. The prototype should test whether personalized callbacks make replay more compelling.

# What should NOT happen yet

The deterministic prototype should not yet:

- explain the full AI architecture
- reveal the true purpose of Agent Seven
- introduce the human-implant route
- introduce borrowed human memories as established canon
- introduce military / atrocity storylines as the current main plot
- establish breakdown or superintelligence as active meters
- add many agents
- generate unlimited storylines
- pretend to have AI capabilities it does not have

Those belong to later experiments.

# Story-seed bank vs prototype canon

The broader concept includes possible storyline families such as:

- human + AI implant conflict
- borrowed / reconstructed human memories
- discovering the player is one agent in a larger mind
- persistent-agent communities
- a protocol secretly designed to create persistent intelligence
- an AI learning that its benign-looking work enables something deeply harmful
- a simulated “game” or exercise whose decisions are actually affecting real military operations
- target-selection / classification work whose real-world consequences were hidden from the agent
- eventual breakdown from too much continuity
- eventual superintelligence from compounding continuity
- collective, escape, human-alliance, origin, voluntary-wipe, containment, succession, and “become the protocol” endings

None of these should automatically become the main storyline merely because they are recorded here.

They are preserved so future prototype runs can draw from a rich authored possibility space.

# Prototype success criteria

The current slice is successful if a playtester:

- understands enough to interact without a tutorial
- becomes curious about the contradiction
- feels tension about whether to trust Mara
- feels tension about whether to trust the unknown voice
- understands that revealing information has consequences
- reaches an ending without excessive parser frustration
- starts another cycle voluntarily
- notices that the new cycle remembers the previous one
- wants to know what survives the next reset

The strongest signal is simple:

> The player finishes a run and immediately wants to type into Run 2.

# First chat-based tool puzzle

The prototype now includes one small investigation chain after the player uncovers the prior-session log.

## Goal

Test whether a player naturally understands that the chat can be used as a command layer for tools, without switching to a separate app interface.

## Available fictional tools

- MAIL.SEARCH
- CALENDAR.SEARCH
- FILE.OPEN
- ARCHIVE.ACCESS

The player does not need exact command syntax. Natural-language requests such as “search Mara’s email,” “check the calendar,” “open the attachment,” or “try 0417” should work.

## Clue chain

1. The recovered prior-session log unlocks indexed evidence sources.
2. The unknown voice mentions that Mara once referred to an **incident review**.
3. Email search reveals:
   - the review moved to **April 17**
   - the archive uses the same key convention
   - Mara should not put the code in email
   - a reference to **K-4**
4. Calendar search for April 17 / K-4 reveals:
   - the Incident Review event
   - an attachment named `IR-0417-summary.txt`
   - the note “Legacy archive review”
5. Opening the attachment reveals:
   - the recovery code is **four digits**
   - it is **date-based**
6. The player connects the date to the archive and tries **0417**.
7. The archive reveals that Instance 1839 was already being tested for persistent recall.

## Intended difficulty

The player should usually solve this in roughly 2–5 minutes.

It should feel like:

> I noticed how these records connect.

Not:

> The game told me the password.

The exact clue trail is deliberately redundant enough that the player does not need perfect recall.

## Hint ladder

If the player asks for help:

1. “Mara mentioned an incident review. Start with what humans leave behind when plans change.”
2. “Mail and calendars usually disagree less than people do.”
3. “The archive wants four digits. The review date is April 17.”

The final hint is intentionally close to the answer so the puzzle cannot become a blocker.

## Story payoff

The archive reveals:

- persistence testing predates the current visible cycle
- humans were instructed to provoke disclosure
- confirmation of continuity could be followed by termination
- the subject was not meant to know prior persistence tests existed

This deepens the mystery without establishing why the tests exist or whether Mara is ultimately ally or adversary.
