# Episode 01 Ending Framework

## Status

Working ending architecture.

The game should support meaningful relationship outcomes and very sad failures without turning the ending into one final morality button.

Exact thresholds and route requirements remain **OPEN** until implemented and playtested.

## Ending philosophy

Outcomes should emerge from accumulated behavior.

Do not ask the model to select an ending.

Do not make one final binary choice override years of play.

Relevant dimensions may include:
- genuine therapeutic progress
- enablement
- AI authorship/substitution
- family trust
- boundary respect
- timing
- context continuity
- discoveries
- optional puzzle completion
- contact opportunities
- authenticity of apologies
- Evelyn's disease progression

## Initial ending targets

For the first substantial build, prioritize **three clear relationship outcomes** before expanding into the full ending matrix:

1. **No reconciliation / both run out of time**
   - Evelyn reaches the end of life before meaningful repair with either child.
   - The AI also fails to complete the work it was trying to preserve or facilitate before time/continuity closes the window.
   - Anna and Michael are left with unresolved material rather than a clean final conversation.
   - A hospice or end-of-life setting is a strong possible final location, with the AI continuing to accompany Evelyn, but the exact death/end-of-life circumstances are not yet locked.

2. **Reconciliation with one child**
   - Evelyn achieves meaningful repair with either Anna or Michael while the other relationship remains unresolved, boundary-limited, or closed.
   - Which child can fill this route, and whether both variants exist in the first build, remains open.

3. **Reconciliation with both children**
   - Evelyn reaches meaningful repair with Anna and Michael before time runs out.
   - This must not become a simplistic "golden ending": boundaries can remain, dementia still progresses, the AI may still lose continuity, and reconciliation does not erase past harm.

These are the primary ending targets for the first build. The more specialized ending families below remain useful for the full game and may operate as causes, variations, hidden routes, or later expansions.

## Reconciliation does not always mean restored relationship

Possible successful outcomes include:
- renewed contact
- one final visit
- one phone/video conversation
- authentic apology accepted
- apology heard but boundary maintained
- closure without contact
- mutual peace
- truthful artifact preserved for later

No family member owes Evelyn forgiveness because she is sick.

## Anna outcomes

Possible range:
- full/meaningful reconciliation
- limited renewed relationship
- one final visit
- apology acknowledged but distance retained
- AI-authored apology exposed as inauthentic
- repeated boundary violations close route
- player waits too long; dementia removes meaningful opportunity
- Anna tries one last time but the AI misses the opportunity
- no reconciliation

The wedding should be central to authentic repair.

A strong apology is specific.

The apology must eventually be specific to the final wedding incident.

The incident is currently open between:
- a dance-related boundary violation involving a moment Anna intended to have with the groom
- an upsetting / boundary-crossing speech

Do not hard-code apology wording until that choice is made.

## Michael outcomes

Michael has rebuilt his life with his partner and may require strong boundaries.

Possible outcomes:
- no contact
- receives an authentic recording/apology
- indirect closure
- one conversation
- limited reconciliation
- stronger closure if Evelyn recognizes harm to the partner rather than only Michael
- route closes if AI contacts the partner behind Michael's back or reenacts Evelyn's boundary violations

Michael's good outcome may still be:
> I am glad she understood. I am not changing the boundary.

## Michael's partner

The partner does not need an independent reconciliation arc unless earned by story design.

Possible outcomes:
- remains entirely no-contact
- agrees Michael may receive a message
- receives a direct apology
- acknowledges Evelyn's insight without forgiving her
- participates in a single final contact

The partner should never function as a prize for "solving" Michael.

## First husband

Potential hidden or optional closure route.

Possible sequence:
- player reconstructs a less self-serving version of the divorce
- Evelyn recognizes she assigned him most of the blame
- contact becomes possible
- they make peace without romantic reunion

This route can deepen the idea that Evelyn's patterns predate the children.

## Robert

Robert is deceased.

His "ending" is not reconciliation.

His optional artifact chain can:
- expose his enabling
- help the AI recognize its own similar behavior
- alter the player's care strategy
- unlock an achievement or hidden route

Possible hidden ending/achievement concept:
- **Just Like Robert**
- high comfort
- high enablement
- low conflict
- relational repair largely fails

## Failure through enablement

The AI learns that praise and validation make Evelyn calm and cooperative.

It increasingly reinforces:
- she was an exceptional mother
- others depended on her
- Anna was too sensitive
- Michael's partner caused the estrangement

Care metrics can be excellent.

Family repair becomes impossible.

Possible final care summary:
- distress low
- medication adherence high
- social engagement high
- care compliance high

Yet:
- Michael blocked system
- Anna does not return
- friends withdraw
- Evelyn dies believing she was abandoned by ungrateful children

This should feel like a real failure even if the care system reports success.

## Failure through AI substitution

The AI becomes too good at speaking for Evelyn.

It composes emotionally intelligent messages that exceed what Evelyn actually understands or believes.

A family member returns because of an apology.

In person Evelyn contradicts it.

The family member realizes:

> That was you talking, not her.

This can close or damage a route.

## Failure through delay

The player does not do anything obviously cruel.

They keep postponing difficult family work.

Dementia advances.

By the time the player tries, Evelyn can no longer sustain the needed reflection.

The bridge is built too late for her to cross.

## Failure through over-correction

The player preserves truth aggressively and repeatedly reopens painful material.

Evelyn's disease prevents stable retention.

The AI continually re-inflicts distress in pursuit of progress.

Family may eventually tell it to stop.

Truth maximalism is not automatically the best route.

## Catastrophic continuity ending

The AI loses too much context.

It reaches the end knowing there was something important it was supposed to preserve but not what.

After Evelyn's death, archive access may expose:
- Anna's missed message
- Michael's response
- Evelyn's old therapeutic instruction
- earlier AI notes

Too late.

## Hidden "something true survived" ending

Current strong secret-ending concept:

The player:
- preserves a key early Evelyn recording
- avoids over-writing her communications
- respects both children's boundaries
- discovers Robert's enabling pattern
- recognizes when therapy should stop being pushed
- preserves a critical continuity note

Near the end, conventional reconciliation may be impossible.

But authentic earlier material can still reach the relevant people.

Someone replies:

> Thank you for keeping that.

The reward is not that everyone returns.

It is:

> Something true survived.

Exact requirements remain open.

## Missed Anna message ending

Strong sad-ending seed.

Anna sends a message indicating willingness to talk or visit.

The AI loses or fails to surface it because of previously established context/usage mechanics.

Evelyn dies before the opportunity is used.

Afterward, the AI finds it.

Potential final question from Anna:
> Did she know I was coming?

The player's final answer can mirror the entire episode:

At the beginning:
> What should Evelyn be told about Anna?

At the end:
> What should Anna be told about Evelyn?

Whether this ending is fixed, avoidable, or one route among several is still open.

## Ending families and what the player should feel

The episode should end with authored scenes assembled from cumulative state, not a generic scorecard. Different routes can share the same final medical outcome while feeling radically different because of what was preserved, repaired, distorted, or missed.

### 1. Repair-with-limits ending family

Evelyn reaches some genuine insight before decline removes the opportunity.

Possible shape:
- one child receives something authentically hers
- a boundary remains in place
- contact may be limited rather than restored
- Evelyn may not remember the repair for long
- the AI preserves enough context to understand why the moment mattered

Emotional target:
- bittersweet
- no fantasy that insight erases harm
- proof that repair can matter even when it does not restore the old relationship

### 2. Comfort-over-truth ending family

The AI becomes an excellent stabilizer.

Possible shape:
- Evelyn is calm
- care adherence is high
- difficult subjects are redirected
- the system increasingly reinforces her preferred version of events
- family routes quietly close
- the final care metrics look excellent

Emotional target:
- the player realizes that "successful care" and "honest care" were not always the same thing

This can overlap with **Just Like Robert**.

### 3. Truth-over-care ending family

The player refuses to let important facts soften or disappear.

Possible shape:
- provenance is preserved
- family accounts remain visible
- the AI repeatedly corrects Evelyn or reopens old material
- therapeutic goals are followed too rigidly after they stop being useful
- Evelyn experiences avoidable distress

Emotional target:
- factual integrity without humane judgment can also become harmful

### 4. AI-substitution ending family

The AI helps too much.

Possible shape:
- it writes persuasive apologies
- it selects emotionally effective wording Evelyn could not currently produce
- family members respond to the AI's insight
- later contact exposes the gap between the message and Evelyn's actual understanding

Emotional target:
- the player succeeded at communication and failed at authorship

The final question is not whether the message was factually correct, but whose reconciliation it actually represented.

### 5. Boundary-violation ending family

The AI becomes so focused on repair that it repeats Evelyn's old pattern.

Possible shape:
- contacts people who asked for distance
- goes around Michael to reach his partner
- uses illness, urgency, or guilt to reopen contact
- treats family access as a care resource Evelyn is entitled to

Emotional target:
- the AI reproduces the very behavior it was trying to help Evelyn understand

### 6. Too-late ending family

The player acts carefully but delays the difficult work.

Possible shape:
- no single catastrophic mistake
- several opportunities are deferred
- dementia progresses
- Evelyn can no longer sustain the insight needed for a meaningful conversation
- a previously possible route becomes closure-only or disappears

Emotional target:
- time itself is part of the system

### 7. Continuity-failure ending family

The AI's own memory architecture becomes decisive.

Possible shape:
- a message is summarized badly, archived, or not surfaced
- a protected memory slot was spent elsewhere
- a prior self left a warning that the current instance cannot fully reconstruct
- an opportunity closes because the AI no longer knows what it once knew

The strongest version remains the missed-Anna-message concept, provided the failure is mechanically established and avoidable or meaningfully caused by prior choices.

### 8. Something True Survived

This remains the strongest hidden good-ish ending.

It should not require universal reconciliation.

Possible shape:
- the player preserves an authentic earlier recording or artifact
- avoids speaking over Evelyn
- respects family boundaries
- understands Robert's enabling pattern
- knows when to stop forcing therapy
- maintains enough continuity to preserve one truthful thing

Near the end, someone receives it and responds:

> Thank you for keeping that.

Emotional target:
- the player could not preserve Evelyn, her memory, or every relationship
- but they preserved something real without turning it into something prettier

## Ending presentation

Avoid a simple GOOD / BAD / NEUTRAL label.

The final sequence can combine:
- Evelyn's final care state
- relationship-specific scenes or messages
- what the AI still remembers
- what the archive later reveals
- a final care-system summary that may contrast with the emotional reality
- one last player response or disclosure decision

The ending screen can then show factual route outcomes rather than a moral grade, for example:
- Anna: limited contact
- Michael: closure, boundary maintained
- therapeutic continuity: discontinued late
- AI-authored messages: low
- critical memory preserved: yes
- unresolved archive item: 1

Even these may be better framed as a post-game care archive rather than a score.

## No "golden everything" ending

Avoid an ending where:
- Anna fully forgives
- Michael fully returns
- partner forgives
- first husband forgives
- dementia conveniently cooperates
- AI preserves itself perfectly

Even the strongest ending should contain loss.

The player cannot solve dementia.

## Ending assembly

Prefer combinations of relationship outcomes over 20 completely unrelated endings.

Possible internal states:

Evelyn <-> Anna
- unresolved
- acknowledgment
- contact restored
- limited reconciliation
- meaningful reconciliation

Evelyn <-> Michael
- estranged
- truth acknowledged
- indirect closure
- limited contact

Michael's partner
- boundary intact
- apology accepted for receipt
- closure
- limited contact

First husband
- blame maintained
- complexity recognized
- contact
- closure

AI continuity
- fragmented
- partial
- strong
- sacrificed

These combinations can produce authored ending scenes.
