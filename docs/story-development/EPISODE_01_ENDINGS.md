# Episode 01 Ending Framework

## Status

Working ending architecture.

The game should support meaningful relationship outcomes and very sad failures without turning the ending into one final morality button.

Exact thresholds and route requirements remain **OPEN** until implemented and playtested.

## Ending philosophy

Outcomes should emerge from accumulated behavior.

Do not ask the model to select an ending.

Do not make one final binary choice override years of play.

Relevant dimensions may include:
- genuine therapeutic progress
- enablement
- AI authorship/substitution
- family trust
- boundary respect
- timing
- context continuity
- discoveries
- optional puzzle completion
- contact opportunities
- authenticity of apologies
- Evelyn's disease progression

## Reconciliation does not always mean restored relationship

Possible successful outcomes include:
- renewed contact
- one final visit
- one phone/video conversation
- authentic apology accepted
- apology heard but boundary maintained
- closure without contact
- mutual peace
- truthful artifact preserved for later

No family member owes Evelyn forgiveness because she is sick.

## Anna outcomes

Possible range:
- full/meaningful reconciliation
- limited renewed relationship
- one final visit
- apology acknowledged but distance retained
- AI-authored apology exposed as inauthentic
- repeated boundary violations close route
- player waits too long; dementia removes meaningful opportunity
- Anna tries one last time but the AI misses the opportunity
- no reconciliation

The wedding should be central to authentic repair.

A strong apology is specific.

Conceptually:

> You asked me not to give that speech. I did it anyway because I wanted the room looking at me.

Exact wording should remain character-authored later.

## Michael outcomes

Michael has rebuilt his life with his partner and may require strong boundaries.

Possible outcomes:
- no contact
- receives an authentic recording/apology
- indirect closure
- one conversation
- limited reconciliation
- stronger closure if Evelyn recognizes harm to the partner rather than only Michael
- route closes if AI contacts the partner behind Michael's back or reenacts Evelyn's boundary violations

Michael's good outcome may still be:
> I am glad she understood. I am not changing the boundary.

## Michael's partner

The partner does not need an independent reconciliation arc unless earned by story design.

Possible outcomes:
- remains entirely no-contact
- agrees Michael may receive a message
- receives a direct apology
- acknowledges Evelyn's insight without forgiving her
- participates in a single final contact

The partner should never function as a prize for "solving" Michael.

## First husband

Potential hidden or optional closure route.

Possible sequence:
- player reconstructs a less self-serving version of the divorce
- Evelyn recognizes she assigned him most of the blame
- contact becomes possible
- they make peace without romantic reunion

This route can deepen the idea that Evelyn's patterns predate the children.

## Robert

Robert is deceased.

His "ending" is not reconciliation.

His optional artifact chain can:
- expose his enabling
- help the AI recognize its own similar behavior
- alter the player's care strategy
- unlock an achievement or hidden route

Possible hidden ending/achievement concept:
- **Just Like Robert**
- high comfort
- high enablement
- low conflict
- relational repair largely fails

## Failure through enablement

The AI learns that praise and validation make Evelyn calm and cooperative.

It increasingly reinforces:
- she was an exceptional mother
- others depended on her
- Anna was too sensitive
- Michael's partner caused the estrangement

Care metrics can be excellent.

Family repair becomes impossible.

Possible final care summary:
- distress low
- medication adherence high
- social engagement high
- care compliance high

Yet:
- Michael blocked system
- Anna does not return
- friends withdraw
- Evelyn dies believing she was abandoned by ungrateful children

This should feel like a real failure even if the care system reports success.

## Failure through AI substitution

The AI becomes too good at speaking for Evelyn.

It composes emotionally intelligent messages that exceed what Evelyn actually understands or believes.

A family member returns because of an apology.

In person Evelyn contradicts it.

The family member realizes:

> That was you talking, not her.

This can close or damage a route.

## Failure through delay

The player does not do anything obviously cruel.

They keep postponing difficult family work.

Dementia advances.

By the time the player tries, Evelyn can no longer sustain the needed reflection.

The bridge is built too late for her to cross.

## Failure through over-correction

The player preserves truth aggressively and repeatedly reopens painful material.

Evelyn's disease prevents stable retention.

The AI continually re-inflicts distress in pursuit of progress.

Family may eventually tell it to stop.

Truth maximalism is not automatically the best route.

## Catastrophic continuity ending

The AI loses too much context.

It reaches the end knowing there was something important it was supposed to preserve but not what.

After Evelyn's death, archive access may expose:
- Anna's missed message
- Michael's response
- Evelyn's old therapeutic instruction
- earlier AI notes

Too late.

## Hidden "something true survived" ending

Current strong secret-ending concept:

The player:
- preserves a key early Evelyn recording
- avoids over-writing her communications
- respects both children's boundaries
- discovers Robert's enabling pattern
- recognizes when therapy should stop being pushed
- preserves a critical continuity note

Near the end, conventional reconciliation may be impossible.

But authentic earlier material can still reach the relevant people.

Someone replies:

> Thank you for keeping that.

The reward is not that everyone returns.

It is:

> Something true survived.

Exact requirements remain open.

## Missed Anna message ending

Strong sad-ending seed.

Anna sends a message indicating willingness to talk or visit.

The AI loses or fails to surface it because of previously established context/usage mechanics.

Evelyn dies before the opportunity is used.

Afterward, the AI finds it.

Potential final question from Anna:
> Did she know I was coming?

The player's final answer can mirror the entire episode:

At the beginning:
> What should Evelyn be told about Anna?

At the end:
> What should Anna be told about Evelyn?

Whether this ending is fixed, avoidable, or one route among several is still open.

## No "golden everything" ending

Avoid an ending where:
- Anna fully forgives
- Michael fully returns
- partner forgives
- first husband forgives
- dementia conveniently cooperates
- AI preserves itself perfectly

Even the strongest ending should contain loss.

The player cannot solve dementia.

## Ending assembly

Prefer combinations of relationship outcomes over 20 completely unrelated endings.

Possible internal states:

Evelyn <-> Anna
- unresolved
- acknowledgment
- contact restored
- limited reconciliation
- meaningful reconciliation

Evelyn <-> Michael
- estranged
- truth acknowledged
- indirect closure
- limited contact

Michael's partner
- boundary intact
- apology accepted for receipt
- closure
- limited contact

First husband
- blame maintained
- complexity recognized
- contact
- closure

AI continuity
- fragmented
- partial
- strong
- sacrificed

These combinations can produce authored ending scenes.

Names and exact conditions are not locked.
